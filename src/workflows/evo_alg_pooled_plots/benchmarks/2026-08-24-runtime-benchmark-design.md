# Runtime benchmark analysis — design

Date: 2026-08-24

Harness for the runtime benchmark of the two sequence-optimizing algorithms of the
*Evolution* package. Written before the numbers were interpreted, so that the
choice of statistics is not a reaction to the result.

## Where the data comes from

Generated by `write_run_script.benchmark_runtime_script` in
`/home/gernot/Code/PhD_Code/Evolution`, run by the four array jobs under
`ARCs/dream/assays/Evolution_runs/protocols/benchmark/`, results under
`ARCs/dream/assays/Evolution_runs/dataset/benchmark/`.

Two trees of the same sweep exist, selected by `USE_MIXED_HARDWARE_DATA` in
`runtime_benchmark.py`:

- **mixed hardware** (`dataset/benchmark/{GA,GA_GPU,greedy,greedy_GPU}`) — the four
  array jobs above. Different cells may have run on different compute nodes, and
  the GPU cells shared their devices. Holds the only CPU cells.
- **same hardware** (`dataset/benchmark/same_hardware/gpu`, protocol
  `protocols/benchmark/260825_180334_bench_runtime_gpu_same_hardware`) — one job
  running every config in sequence on one node with one uncontended GPU. GPU only
  so far; both algorithms share the one `gpu` folder. This is the default.

30 synthetic sequences of 3020 bp (1500 random bases, 20 `N`, 1500 random bases),
each with a VCF of 300 biallelic SNPs at distinct non-`N` positions. Scoring uses a
single deepCRE model rather than an ensemble, mutations are allowed over the whole
sequence, and checkpointing is off, so the measured time is the algorithm rather
than file IO.

Sweep cells:

| algorithm | swept | levels | fixed |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ga` | population size | 25, 50, 100, 200 | 1000 generations |
| `ga` | generations | 50, 200, 2000 | population 100 |
| `ga` | mutation cap | 2999 (control) | population 100, 1000 generations, unconstrained only |
| `greedy` | mutation cap | 5, 10, 20, 40, 100 | — |

Every cell except the cap control is run twice: `unconstrained` (any base may be
mutated) and `natural` (only the VCF's 300 SNPs). Four device folders — `GA`,
`GA_GPU`, `greedy`, `greedy_GPU` — give 50 cells and 1500 timed runs.

## Data-model decisions

**Wall time only.** `algorithm_seconds` is the sole runtime measure.
`model_load_seconds` stays in the tidy CSV but enters no comparison: it ranges
0.25 s to 19 s depending on whether the process was first in its folder, so it is
cold-start noise rather than algorithm cost.

**No derived evaluation count for greedy.** `run_greedy_algorithm` writes no
`n_evaluations`, and reconstructing it would require assuming how many candidate
mutations each step scores. Greedy is therefore compared on wall time alone and
appears in no cost-per-evaluation figure — and, per the same decision, in no
head-to-head figure against the genetic algorithm. Greedy gets its own figures.

**Folder names are validated, not trusted.** The sweep cell is recorded only in the
run-folder name, so a mislabelled or misplaced folder would silently pool runs of
different settings. Two checks catch that: the swept config field inside
`timings.json` must equal the value in the label (`pop50` ⇒ `population_size == 50`),
and for the genetic algorithm `n_evaluations` must equal
`population_size * (number_of_generations + 1)`, the identity the algorithm uses to
write it. Both raise rather than warn.

**Missing cells raise.** A run folder without `timings.json`, or a missing device
folder, is an error. `check_expected_cells` additionally reports cells and
sequences per (algorithm, device) against the 15/10 the sweep defines, so a cell
that failed on the cluster is visible as a shortfall rather than as a quietly
thinner boxplot.

## Statistics

The 30 sequences of a cell are the replicates. Their runtime distributions are
described by median and interquartile range, not mean and standard deviation:
runtimes are bounded below and occasionally long-tailed.

No significance testing. These are deterministic timings of one implementation, not
samples from a population; a p-value would answer a question nobody asked.

Scaling is summarized by a power law. Writing $t$ for the measured wall time in
seconds, $p$ for the swept parameter (population size, generation count, mutation
cap, or model evaluation count), $a$ for the exponent and $c$ for the coefficient:

$$ t = c \, p^{a} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \log t = \log c + a \log p $$

so $a$ and $\log c$ are the slope and intercept of an ordinary least-squares fit of
$\log t$ on $\log p$. Runtime linear in the parameter gives $a = 1$; quadratic gives
$a = 2$. Every sequence of every level enters the fit, so with $n$ points the
interval on the exponent is $a \pm t_{1-\alpha/2,\,n-2}\, s_a$, where $s_a$ is the
standard error of the slope and $t_{1-\alpha/2,\,n-2}$ the two-sided critical value
of Student's $t$ distribution with $n-2$ degrees of freedom at confidence level
$1-\alpha$ (0.95 by default).

Worked example, the CPU/natural population sweep: median wall times 292.6, 480.9,
810.5 and 1505.9 s at populations 25, 50, 100 and 200. Doubling the population
multiplies the time by 1.64, 1.69 and 1.86, i.e. by less than 2, so the exponent is
below 1; the fit over all 120 runs gives $a = 0.79$ with $R^2 = 0.98$.

**That interval assumes the residuals are noise.** When the measured curve is
systematically not a power law, the interval is far too narrow and the exponent is
an artefact of forcing a line through a bend. Two diagnostics guard against quoting
such a number, and both are written out and printed:

- `find_non_monotonic_curves` reports any step where the median *falls* as the
  swept parameter grows. Every parameter here adds work, so a fall reports
  something other than the swept parameter.
- `flag_poor_power_law_fits` reports curves with $R^2$ below
  `POWER_LAW_R_SQUARED_FLOOR` (0.95).

Both run over `cost_sweep_rows`, which drops one thing only: the genetic
algorithm's `maxmut2999` cell. The mutation cap means different things to the two
algorithms — for the genetic algorithm it is a control that is *expected* to change
nothing, so fitting across it or demanding monotonicity would be meaningless, while
for greedy it is the number of optimization steps and hence its only cost driver.

`regime_runtime_ratio` gives the natural-over-unconstrained median ratio per cell.
For the genetic algorithm the two regimes run the *same* number of model
evaluations, so this ratio isolates the cost of the constraint bookkeeping. For
greedy the regimes also differ in candidates per step, so its ratio mixes both.

## Layout

- `runtime_benchmark_calc.py` — loading, validation, summaries, fits. No plotting.
- `runtime_benchmark_plot.py` — `plot_runtime_sweep` (sweep boxplots) and
  `plot_scaling_fit` (log–log scatter with the fitted laws). No statistics, no
  filtering. Both follow the package's ax-injection convention, so either can
  become a panel of a composed paper figure.
- `runtime_benchmark.py` — thin orchestration: loads, writes the CSVs, draws the
  figures into `benchmarks_mixed_hardware/` (gitignored).

Filtering lives in the orchestration, not in the plotting: the same two functions
serve every figure and any future panel.

**Plot type follows the number of levels.** `plot_sweep` dispatches on
`swept_value.nunique()`: above `MAX_LEVELS_FOR_BOXPLOT` (2) a sweep is drawn as a
log–log scatter of the individual sequences with one fitted power law per regime,
the exponent, its interval and $R^2$ in the legend. Every parameter swept here is a
quantity, so the question is how runtime *scales*, and a fitted exponent answers it
where a row of boxes leaves the reader eyeballing a slope. Boxplots survive only at
two levels — the mutation-cap control — where there is no scaling to fit and the
replicate spread is what decides whether the two cells differ.

Putting the fit in the figure also puts its failure there: the unconstrained GA
population curve shows its population-50 cluster sitting below both the fit line and
the population-25 cluster, with $R^2$ 0.656 in the legend, so a reader sees the
misfit without opening `unfittable_curves.csv`.

**One device per figure.** CPU and GPU never share axes. Each device gets the same
full set — the two GA sweeps, the GA cap control, the greedy cap sweep and the GA
evaluation-scaling plot — and GPU titles carry `(contended)`. Putting a contended
GPU box next to a clean CPU box invites reading a speedup off a number that does
not support one.

**Hue-coloured box outlines.** Most cells here have an interquartile range of a few
seconds against a median of hundreds, so on a logarithmic axis the box collapses to
a hairline and seaborn's uniform grey outline erases the hue. `color_box_outlines`
repaints each box's edge, median, whiskers and caps in that box's own colour and
lightens the fill, so a collapsed box still reads as its regime. Artists are matched
to their box by horizontal centre, not by seaborn's drawing order, which leaves the
replicate dots untouched. The same idea is implemented separately in
`natural_unconstrained_comparison/region_mutation_breakdown.py`; promoting one
version to a shared plotting module is an open follow-up.

## Known caveats of this dataset

1. **The GA mutation cap is 20, not the 90 the current `write_run_script.py`
   writes.** The data predates that change. `realized_mutations` therefore sits at
   the cap in nearly every GA cell, so this benchmark says nothing about GA
   solution quality and the algorithms can only be compared at depth 20.
2. **GPU cells shared devices.** Both GPU array jobs request `gpu=1` per task with
   `-tc 15`. GPU wall times are contended upper bounds; every GPU figure is its
   own, marked `(contended)`. A clean number needs `-tc 1`. The greedy GPU sweep
   shows the contention directly: cap 100 comes out *faster* than cap 40 in both
   regimes, though it strictly does more work.
3. **The unconstrained GA population sweep is not monotonic**: population 25 is
   reproducibly slower than population 50 on both devices, with interquartile
   ranges of a few seconds. Its power-law exponent is not interpretable; both
   diagnostics flag it. The mechanism is not established — a batch-size threshold
   in the model call would fit, but nothing here tests that.
