Transcription-factor binding under sequence optimization

What the evolutionary optimizer does to predicted TF binding, for two new analyses: a random-start run and a 2×2 gene-source × fitness-model comparison.

Arabidopsis (ara) and Zea mays (zea) deepCRE (MSR) expression models · generated 2026-07-02

How the data was generated

An evolutionary algorithm mutates gene promoter sequences to maximize predicted expression, where the fitness score comes from a deepCRE expression model. Each run therefore gives, per sequence, a reference (starting) promoter and an optimized promoter.

A separate model (deepCIS sliding-window scan → peak calling) counts, for each promoter, how many binding peaks each transcription-factor (TF) family has. Per sequence and TF we take

diff = peak_count(optimized) − peak_count(reference)

so a positive diff means the optimizer introduced that TF's binding and a negative one means it removed it. Sequences are the replicates. Per-TF significance is a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test of the per-sequence diff against zero, with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction across TFs (stars: * q<0.05, ** q<0.01, *** q<0.001). Heatmap cells show the mean diff per sequence; red = binding introduced, blue = removed.

The two analyses differ only in which runs feed this pipeline:

  • Random-start: one run of 104 random starting sequences (not natural genes) maximized under the Arabidopsis model.
  • 2×2 model comparison: natural promoters from two species — 105 Arabidopsis genes and 100 Zea mays genes — each optimized under both the ara model and the zea model (four runs, all maximizing). Within a gene source the two runs share the same genes, so the model swap is paired by gene; the wild-type reference scan is model-independent (verified: identical reference counts), so the model contrast is a clean optimized(ara model) − optimized(zea model).

1. Random-start run

Starting from random sequence, which TF families does the optimizer systematically recruit or remove to raise predicted expression? With 104 sequences as replicates, 12 of 36 TF families change significantly (q<0.05).

Takeaway. To make a random sequence "look expressed," the optimizer consistently adds GC-rich / activator-type motifs (C2C2gata, Trihelix, LOBAS2, G2like, C3H) and strips out bZIP, bHLH and NAC binding. The removals are the strongest signals of all.

2. 2×2 gene-source × model comparison

Does the choice of predictor model drive which TFs the optimizer targets? The figure groups the four runs by model (left = ara model, right = zea model) and orders rows by mean(ara model) − mean(zea model). Rows at the top are recruited more strongly under the ara model; rows at the bottom under the zea model.

Takeaway. The fitness model clearly shapes the TF strategy, but mostly in a gene-source-specific way. Sign convention below: D = ara model − zea model, so D<0 means the zea model introduces the TF more strongly.

Model main effect (pooled over both gene sources)

Pooling all 205 genes, 12 of 40 TFs show a significant overall model effect. The zea model recruits C3H (q≈3×10−15), LOBAS2 and Trihelix much more strongly, while the ara model favors CPP, C2C2gata and ARF. The single largest cell in the heatmap is LOBAS2 under the zea model on Arabidopsis genes (+1.26 peaks/gene vs +0.15 under the ara model).

Within each gene source (paired contrasts)

  • Arabidopsis genes: 16 / 40 TFs differ between models. Zea-model-driven: LOBAS2, Trihelix, C3H (each D=−1); ara-model-driven: CPP, C2C2gata (D=+1).
  • Zea genes: only 8 / 40 differ — the model matters less on zea genes. Strongest: C3H (zea-model-driven), FAR1, bHLH.

So "which model" and "which genes" are not independent: most of the model-driven TF changes are conditional on the gene source, and the model effect is generally larger on Arabidopsis genes than on zea genes.

Cross-cutting note. Several families recur across all analyses. C2C2gata, Trihelix, LOBAS2 and C3H are the optimizer's go-to "activating" motifs (introduced from random sequence, and model-dependent on natural genes), whereas bHLH / bZIP / NAC are the ones it removes. That the same handful of families dominate every comparison suggests the deepCRE models lean on a compact motif vocabulary.
